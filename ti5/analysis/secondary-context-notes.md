### Part 2: Disclosure (Ringkasan pp. 10–15)
- **Protective → Informative evolution**: SEC shifted from protective to informative disclosure over time. SEC ASR 190 mandated replacement cost data for large firms.
- **Differential disclosure**: 10-K and 10-Q used by professional analysts; differential ≠ selective
- **Regulation FD (2000)**: Outlaws selective disclosure to analysts before public announcement. Research results mixed (Irani/Karamanou, Bushee/Matsumoto/Miller, Heflin/Subramanyan/Zhang)
- **Lev argument for expanded disclosure**: Market becomes "thin" without info equality; disclosure benefits all users; companies should have organized disclosure policies aligned with production, marketing, investment
- **Signaling theory**: Good news → price up; no disclosure → assumed bad news → price down

### Part 3: SFAS 131 Section (Ringkasan pp. 13–14) ← Most relevant to TI5

**Section 4.2 Pengungkapan Segmen – SFAS No. 131:**

Core contrast:
- SFAS 14: "pilihan luas... termasuk produk, proses produksi, dan saluran pemasaran" — flexible, managerially-defined industries
- SFAS 131: "pelaporan segmen berdasarkan 'pendekatan manajemen… didasarkan pada cara manajemen mengatur segmen-segmen dalam perusahaan untuk membuat keputusan operasional dan menilai kinerja'"

Quantitative thresholds SFAS 131:
- ≥ 10% total revenue (internal + external)
- ≥ 10% total profit of all operating segments
- ≥ 10% total assets of all reporting segments
- 75% floor: at least 75% of consolidated revenue must be in reported segments

Empirical evidence:
- Street, Nichols & Gray: fewer single-segment claims, more segments reported, more items per segment under SFAS 131
- Hermann & Thomas: 68/100 companies changed segment definitions (double-edged — some may be hiding information)
- Recent research: higher predictive power (current revenue → next year revenue) under SFAS 131 vs SFAS 14
- **Explicit conclusion**: "SFAS No. 131 jelas merupakan peningkatan dibandingkan SFAS No. 14"

**Summary table from Ringkasan:**
| Bentuk | Poin Penting |
|--------|-------------|
| Pengungkapan Segmen (SFAS 131) | Pendekatan manajemen (tidak kaku seperti SFAS No. 14). Ambang batas 10%. Target 75% pendapatan konsolidasi. Hasil: jumlah segmen meningkat. |

### Part 4: Case Study — PT Mayora Indah Tbk (MYOR)
- Company: FMCG manufacturer; net sales ≈ Rp 38.68T; net profit ≈ Rp 2.86T (down 4.5%); total assets ≈ Rp 31.37T
- Segments: makanan olahan, minuman olahan; pasar domestik dan ekspor
- **Segment disclosure limitation**: "pengungkapan segmen usaha masih bersifat umum (hanya makanan dan minuman)" — only broad food/beverage split, not granular enough for sophisticated analysis
- Analogy to ICBP/PSAK 5 issue: aggregate reporting obscures profitability differences
