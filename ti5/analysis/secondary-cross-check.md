## What TI5 should add beyond Group 4

### Theoretical depth
1. **Decision usefulness framing** — connect SFAS 131 improvement to Scott's decision usefulness framework: information is useful if it changes beliefs and affects decisions. SFAS 14's freedom to define industries meant information disclosed was not necessarily what investors needed to form beliefs about economic performance.
2. **CODM concept explicitly** — Chief Operating Decision Maker as the organizer of segment information; management approach aligns external disclosure with internal reporting, reducing information asymmetry structurally (not just behaviorally)
3. **Finite uniformity lens** — SFAS 131 is finite uniformity applied to disclosure: it requires different segment structures for different organizational realities, rather than imposing a single industry taxonomy. SFAS 14 was closer to flexibility — managers had broad freedom to define industries without identified relevant circumstances.

### Empirical evidence structure
- Cite Street/Nichols/Gray more precisely: comparison 1997 (SFAS 14) vs 1998 (SFAS 131) — this is the most direct evidence
- Hermann/Thomas caveat: 68/100 changed definitions — double-edged evidence (may include companies obscuring info)
- Predictive validity finding: stronger current→next-period revenue relationship under SFAS 131

### PSAK 5 / Indonesian context
- PSAK 5 (Segmen Operasi) adopts management approach from IFRS 8, which is the international equivalent of SFAS 131
- ICBP's 6-segment structure under PSAK 5 illustrates management approach in practice
- ICBP Pinehill aggregation issue shows remaining limitation even under management approach (relevant for critical analysis section)

### Critical balance
TI5 should acknowledge SFAS 131 is not perfect:
- Aggregation allowed (Hermann/Thomas "hiding" concern)
- Management still defines segments — potential for broad aggregation (as seen in ICBP/Pinehill)
- But overall: improvement confirmed empirically and theoretically
