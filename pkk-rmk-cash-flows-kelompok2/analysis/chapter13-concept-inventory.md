## 0. Opening framing & learning objectives — pp. 2
- C-01. Six stated learning objectives (SCFP construction; motivation for FASB's move; SCF requirements + nonarticulation; classification problems in the trichotomy; analytical usefulness; importance of FCF). (p. 2)
- C-02. *The Music Man* motif: "Cash for the merchandise…" — bills, debts, dividends are paid with **cash, not earnings**; insufficient cash ⇒ extinction. (p. 2)
- C-03. Historical arc: APB Opinion No. 19 (1971) mandates the SCFP ("funds flow"); SFAS No. 95 (1987) mandates the SCF, superseding the SCFP — FASB's interest in cash-basis reporting as a **supplement** to accrual statements. (p. 2)

## 1. The Statement of Changes in Financial Position (SCFP) — pp. 3–4
- C-04. Funds flow statement as the SCFP's own predecessor; working capital accounts = fund balance; liquidity emphasis. (p. 3)
- C-05. APB Opinion No. 19's three reporting objectives: (a) complete disclosure of changes in financial position, (b) summarize financing & investing activity, (c) report funds flow from operations. (p. 3)
- C-06. The SCFP as a **derivative financial statement** (relies on element definitions/measurements of the other two statements). (p. 3)
- C-07. **Equation 13.1** (SCFP identity) + two balancing sections: *sources of resources* = transaction credits (equity increases, asset decreases); *uses of resources* = transaction debits (equity decreases, asset increases). (p. 3)
- C-08. **Exhibit 13.1** — standard SCFP format; funds flow statement = points 1a/1b only; adding point 2 ⇒ **all-inclusive / all-resources** SCFP; examples of nonfund transactions (debt conversion, stock for nonmonetary assets, property dividends, nonmonetary exchanges). (pp. 3–4)
- C-09. Four permitted fund definitions under APB 19: cash; cash + near cash; quick assets; working capital — flexibility example; **working capital minimized preparation cost ⇒ most firms chose net working capital**. (p. 4)
- C-10. Gradual recognition timeline: APB Opinion No. 3 (1963, recommended) → SEC mandatory for statutory filings (1971) → APB Opinion No. 19 (1971, mandatory for financial reporting). (p. 4)

## 2. Motivation for a Cash Flow Statement — pp. 4–5
- C-11. Consensus during FASB deliberations: funds = **cash**, not net working capital. (pp. 4–5)
- C-12. Net working capital is a poor liquidity measure — **three reasons**: (1) deferred charges/credits have no cash consequences; (2) conversion of current assets to cash can exceed a year (long operating cycles); (3) inventory at cost does not measure cash flow potential. "Cash is cash is cash." (p. 5)

## 3. Objectives — pp. 5–6
- C-13. SFAC No. 1's three objectives of financial reporting; decision-usefulness; net resources & changes; assessing future cash flows — the 1980s motivation for the SCF. (p. 5)
- C-14. SFAC No. 5's claims for the SCF (cash generation through operations to repay debt/distribute dividends/reinvest; financing activities; investing; assessing liquidity, financial flexibility, profitability, risk). (p. 5)
- C-15. Discussion-memorandum **six-point list**: (1) feedback on actual cash flows; (2) income–cash-flow relationship; (3) quality of income; (4) comparability; (5) flexibility & liquidity; (6) prediction of future cash flows — all addressing limitations of accrual accounting. (p. 5)
- C-16. **Quality of income** concept: the higher the correlation between accounting income and cash flow, the higher the quality of income. (pp. 5–6)
- C-17. Uniformity/comparability argument: CFO subject to fewer arbitrary policy choices than income. (p. 6)
- C-18. Financial flexibility vs liquidity definitions; balance sheet's current–noncurrent system as a **poor guide to liquidity** (deferred items, slow inventory conversion, non-NRV measurement ⇒ "crude ranking of liquidity"). (p. 6)
- C-19. Exit-price accounting (Appendix 1-A ref) as only a crude flexibility indicator — **speed** of conversion governs; firms raise capital incrementally, don't liquidate productive assets. (p. 6)
- C-20. Prediction point: unsettled whether cash flow, funds flow, or income best predicts future cash flows; unclear prediction horizon; expanded-disclosure philosophy. (p. 6)

## 4. Requirements / Structure of the SCF — pp. 7–9
- C-21. SCFP's binary sources/uses framework vs SCF's **operating / investing / financing trichotomy** — more meaningful, more classificational consistency, greater comparability. (p. 7)
- C-22. **Cash and cash equivalents** definition (cash on hand/demand deposits + short-term highly liquid investments convertible into known amounts). (p. 7)
- C-23. All-inclusive/all-resources retained: noncash investing & financing reported as a supplement (schedule or narrative). (p. 7)
- C-24. **Three-of-seven FASB dissent**: interest/dividends received are investing, interest paid is financing (per finance discipline) — contra ¶22–23 of SFAS 95. (p. 7)
- C-25. **Direct vs indirect (reconciliation) method**: direct reports literal cash flows by income statement categories; indirect starts from accrual income and adjusts; direct reports more information and **FASB favors it**; cost argument acknowledged; the un-mentioned **"plug number"** practice under the indirect method. (p. 7)
- C-26. **Exhibit 13.2** — direct-method SCF, Company M, FY 2000. (pp. 7–8)
- C-27. If direct is used, a separate reconciliation schedule is required (**Exhibit 13.3**); indirect must be used alone or as supplement; some FASB members: allowing indirect impedes understanding. (p. 8)
- C-28. Great majority of US firms use the indirect method (cost-driven); both methods give the same CFO; **McEnroe survey**: 282 respondents, **56% direct vs 44% indirect**; trade-off — direct = easy to understand/hard to prepare, indirect = the opposite. (p. 9)

## 5. The Nonarticulation Problem — pp. 9–11
- C-29. **Bahnson, Miller & Budge**: under the indirect method, cash flows from changes in working capital accounts ≠ working capital adjustments in the operating section; discrepancies in **75% of sample**; SCF appears inconsistent with the balance sheet. (p. 9)
- C-30. **Exhibit 13.4** — 3M Company (2005/2004): balance-sheet changes vs SCF working-capital adjustments; nontrivial differences (AR % difference; even signs can differ). (p. 10)
- C-31. Cause 1 — **mid-year acquisitions**: acquired firms' beginning working-capital balances absent from beginning consolidated balance sheets. (p. 10)
- C-32. Cause 2 — noncash working-capital transactions (write-ups/downs on purchase acquisitions; depreciation allocated into manufactured inventories; current/noncurrent reclassifications). (p. 10)
- C-33. Cause 3 — **shared accounts payable**: one AP account used for both operating purchases (inventory) and investing purchases (equipment) ⇒ balance-sheet ΔAP ≠ operating-section AP adjustment. (p. 10)
- C-34. 2008 FASB–IASB discussion paper on presentation formats: more management discretion; questionable improvement; merit of a single "business" classification reducing operating/investing shifting. (pp. 10–11)

## 6. Classification Problems of SFAS No. 95 — pp. 11–13
- C-35. **Nurnberg's critique**: trichotomy claimed to follow finance literature, yet interest/dividends received = operating inflows and interest paid = operating outflow — finance says investing and financing respectively. (p. 11)
- C-36. **Proprietary vs entity theory**: SFAS 95 follows the income-statement (proprietary) orientation; entity approach would classify interest/dividend revenue as investing, interest expense as financing; **banking-industry influence** (banks avoid negative CFO). (p. 11)
- C-37. **IAS 7 flexibility**: interest/dividends received or paid may be operating, investing, or financing if consistent period to period; conflicts with SFAS 95's proprietary orientation; within-industry uniformity if banks classify as operating. (p. 11)
- C-38. **Premium/discount on bonds & notes** — Vent, Cowling & Sevalstad's **four methods** (worked example: 4-yr 8% bonds, $10,000 face sold at $11,000; straight-line amortization; $550 annual interest expense). **Exhibit 13.5**. Authors prefer Method 1; Method 4 makes least sense; another instance of the **allocation problem**. (pp. 11–12)
- C-39. **Capitalized interest (SFAS No. 34)** — excluded from operating, included in investing as part of asset cost (Munter): classify by **basic nature** of the receipt/expenditure vs **ultimate purpose** of the event? (p. 12)
- C-40. **Capital vs operating leases**: operating lease payment wholly operating; capital lease split — interest portion operating, principal reduction financing; nature-vs-purpose question unresolved. (p. 12)
- C-41. **SFAS No. 104** (amends 95): hedging flexibility — hedge classified with the hedged balance-sheet item (operations) or as investing; Nurnberg & Largay: less comparability, possibly justified as increased **"fineness"**; pointer to FASB ASC 230-10-45. (p. 13)

## 7. Analytical Usefulness — pp. 13–14
- C-42. **Ingram & Lee**: income statement + SCF used together. Growing firms: higher income, lower CFO (inventory/AR build, partially offset by payables); investment outflows; financing inflows; low dividends. Contracting firms: reverse. ~1,000 firms, 1974–1992; deductive analysis supported; **expanding firms carry more leverage**. (pp. 13–14)

## 8. Misclassification / Manipulation of Classifications — pp. 14–15
- C-43. SCF less manipulable than income but not exempt; CFO focal ⇒ shifting outflows operating→investing and inflows investing→operating; total cash unchanged but perception improves. (p. 14)
- C-44. **Tyco** (Maremont): purchased dealer contracts treated as "acquisitions" (slower operating outflow recognition) vs internally generated contracts — a case where **rigid uniformity is well-merited**. (p. 14)
- C-45. **Auto-industry examples** (Mulford, WSJ): Ford, GM, Harley-Davidson — dealer notes receivable classified as investing while sales revenue recognized; GM reported CFO $7.6B vs $3.5B if operating. (pp. 14–15)
- C-46. **Navistar contrast**: consolidated results reclassify captive-finance notes receivable into CFO — "we define ourselves as a manufacturing company with a finance subsidiary"; flexibility ⇒ weak comparability even without rule violations. (p. 15)

## 9. The SCF Is More Than CFO — pp. 15–16
- C-47. Undue attention to CFO is problematic; all three sections matter. (p. 15)
- C-48. **WorldCom**: Chapter 11 (July 21, 2002); capitalized operating expenses ⇒ inflated CFO, lent credibility to net income; **Exhibit 13.6** (1998–2001 selected SCF items); CFO − CFI shows cash hemorrhage in 3 of 4 pre-bankruptcy years; cumulative **"$12 billion hole"** by Dec 31, 2001; investors ignoring sections "do so at their peril." (pp. 15–16)

## 10. Cash Flow Needs of Different Users — pp. 16–17
- C-49. **Buffett's three questions** (1988 BRK letter): worth of the company; likelihood of meeting obligations; managerial performance given the hand dealt — i.e., valuation, credit, stewardship; stewardship needs accurate historical cash flows; credit/valuation need unbiased predictions (SFAC No. 1). (p. 16)
- C-50. Capital-budgeting/NPV framing: accept if NPV > 0; stock purchase if per-share PV of expected cash flows (**intrinsic value**) > market price; Buffett's "Owner's Manual" definition of intrinsic value (discounted value of cash takeable out of the business; an estimate, not precise). (pp. 16–17)

## 11. Free Cash Flow — pp. 17–21
- C-51. FCF as the firm-level analog of capital-budgeting cash flows; Mulford & Comiskey: "free" = absence of a superior claim; growing popularity as reaction to earnings-management scandals; several definitions in use; chapter follows **entity theory** (cash flow to the firm). (p. 17)
- C-52. **Equation 13.2**: FCF = NOPLAT − investment in operating invested capital; NOPLAT = net operating profit less adjusted taxes (≈ after-tax operating income); investment split into net operating working capital + noncurrent assets; **FCF excludes interest expense** (a financing expense); **operating cash counts as invested capital**. (p. 17)
- C-53. FCF cannot be obtained directly from the SCF. (p. 17)
- C-54. **ABC Company worked example**: Exhibit 13.7 (income statement + balance sheet, p. 18) → Exhibit 13.8 (SCF, pp. 18–19) → Exhibit 13.9 (statement of FCF, pp. 19–20) → **Exhibit 13.10** (computing FCF from the SCF: add back after-tax interest expense to CFO; subtract increase in operating cash, pp. 20–21). (pp. 17–21)
- C-55. **Exhibit 13.11** — four performance/valuation measures compared (net income → CFO → CFO−CFI → FCF); choice depends on time/resources/use; CFO "contaminated" with interest; intrinsic value = forecasted FCFs discounted at **WACC**; four measures give potentially different views — "the real world is never simple." (pp. 18–21)

## 12. Cash and Funds Flow Research — p. 21
- C-56. **Lawson & Lee**: cash flow reports necessary for performance reporting; Lee: "Cash flow and not profit is the end result of entity activity. Profit is an abstraction; cash is a physical resource." (p. 21)
- C-57. Capital-market evidence: accruals provide information **over and above** literal cash flows ⇒ complementarity — cash flows and accruals more useful together; decomposition provides new information. (p. 21)
- C-58. Surveys: accrual-based profitability analysis still dominates, but FAF-commissioned survey shows funds flow rising / accrual falling in importance. (p. 21)

## 13. Improving the SCF — pp. 21–22
- C-59. **Broome**: SCF crucial to securities analysis; post-Adelphia/Dynegy/Qwest/Tyco/WorldCom it can and should be improved; indirect-method adjustments hard to understand, give managers manipulation leeway, often irreconcilable with balance-sheet changes. (pp. 21–22)
- C-60. **Broome's three recommendations**: (1) require the direct method AND the reconciliation; (2) more classification guidance; (3) reverse the reconciliation (start from CFO, proceed to net income). (p. 22)
- C-61. **Authors' recommendations**: agree direct should be required; if choice is kept — require a schedule of noncash transactions affecting working capital; for mid-year acquisitions, a schedule reconciling operating-section working-capital adjustments to balance-sheet changes; in general, explain any nonarticulation. (p. 22)

## 14. Summary — pp. 22–23
- C-62. SCF as a special case of the SCFP with funds = cash; derivative statement, but new information via decomposition into cash/accrual components and the trichotomy; proprietary choice and its classification consequences; nonarticulation + misclassification recap; IAS flexibility; research supportive; near-unanimous indirect selection; FCF growth (consistent with interest as financing); authors' verdict — SCF brought greater consistency, predictive usefulness, comparability; expected to grow in importance because it lacks the income statement's "arbitrariness." (pp. 22–23)
