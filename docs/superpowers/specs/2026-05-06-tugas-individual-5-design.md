## 4. Section Architecture

### Section I — Pendahuluan

**Purpose**: Frame the question as a problem of informative disclosure design. Establish that SFAS 131 represents a philosophical reconception, not a cosmetic revision.

**Key moves**:
- Open with Wolk's framing of segment disclosure as informative disclosure — aimed at enriching users' risk-return analysis
- Identify the core information gap SFAS 14 created: disconnect between management's knowledge and external users' view
- State thesis explicitly: SFAS 131 improves by (1) anchoring segment definition to CODM's observable internal structure, (2) aligning external with internal reporting, (3) empirical validation

**Citations**: Wolk et al. (2017); Scott (2015) for decision usefulness framing

---

### Section II — SFAS 14 dan Tiga Kelemahannya

**Purpose**: Establish what was broken — definitional, behavioral, and informational failures.

**Kelemahan 1 — Flexibility menyamar sebagai uniformity**
SFAS 14 permitted management to define "industries" using any of several bases (products, production processes, marketing channels) with no identified *relevant circumstances* constraining the choice. In Wolk's framework this is closer to *flexibility* than finite uniformity: freedom without an accountable criterion. Nominal comparability without substantive comparability.

**Kelemahan 2 — Single-segment gaming**
Management's unconstrained definitional freedom allowed companies to claim single-segment status despite genuinely diverse operations. Street, Nichols & Gray (2000) confirm a disproportionate number of companies reported as single-segment under SFAS 14 — inconsistent with economic reality of diversified corporations. Manipulation-by-design: when a standard creates no meaningful constraint, management chooses the definition that minimizes disclosure burden.

**Kelemahan 3 — Disconnect antara eksternal dan internal**
The segment map disclosed under SFAS 14 did not have to correspond to how management actually monitored the business. External users received a classification the company's own leadership may not have used for decisions. This is a fundamental information asymmetry: segment disclosure's core purpose — enabling users to evaluate performance by business unit — was undermined when disclosed units bore no relationship to actual management structure.

**Citations**: Wolk et al. (2017) pp. 252–255; Street, Nichols & Gray (2000)

---

### Section III — SFAS 131: Pendekatan Manajemen sebagai Koreksi Struktural

**Purpose**: Show how each innovation in SFAS 131 directly addresses each of SFAS 14's three failures.

**Inovasi 1 — CODM sebagai anchor definisi segmen**
Segments defined as components about which the CODM makes resource allocation decisions and evaluates performance. Anchors segment definition to an observable organizational fact rather than unconstrained choice. Eliminates flexibility problem by substituting a verifiable internal criterion.

**Inovasi 2 — Keselarasan laporan eksternal dan internal**
Requiring disclosed segments to match internal reporting structurally reduces information asymmetry. Users receive the same decomposition of the business management uses to allocate resources and evaluate managers. Connects to Wolk's definition of informative disclosure: information that enriches risk-return analysis because it is the most decision-relevant decomposition available, as proven by management's own use.

**Inovasi 3 — Ambang batas kuantitatif sebagai finite uniformity**
10%/10%/10% thresholds (revenue, profit, assets) and 75% consolidated revenue floor are bright-line criteria: they constrain aggregation without dictating segment structure. In Wolk's framework, SFAS 131 = *finite uniformity applied to disclosure*: relevant circumstances (organizational structure as reflected in CODM practice) determine boundaries; quantitative thresholds ensure minimum granularity. SFAS 14 provided no equivalent discipline.

**Citations**: Wolk et al. (2017) pp. 255–260; FASB (1997)

---

### Section IV — Bukti Empiris dan Analisis Kritis

**Purpose**: Validate the improvement empirically; surface the aggregation caveat; resolve it.

**Bukti 1 — Street, Nichols & Gray (2000)**
Before/after comparison: 1997 (SFAS 14) vs. 1998 (SFAS 131). Findings:
- Significant decline in single-segment companies
- Average number of reported segments increased
- Items reported per segment increased
Most direct evidence of improvement: same population, one year apart, two different standards.

**Bukti 2 — Predictive validity**
Stronger relationship between current-period segment revenue and next-year revenue under SFAS 131 vs. SFAS 14. Decision-usefulness test: SFAS 131 segment data improves forecast quality — the standard achieves its informative disclosure purpose.

**Catatan kritis — Hermann & Thomas (2000)**
68 of 100 sample companies changed segment definitions after adoption. Double-edged: most changes reflect correct realignment with internal structure, but some companies may have exploited aggregation flexibility to report fewer, broader segments — using the management approach as cover for less granular disclosure. The aggregation loophole: when management genuinely organizes broadly internally, SFAS 131 permits and requires broad reporting.

**Resolusi**
Aggregation caveat is real but does not reverse the verdict. Predictive validity finding operates at population level — confirms SFAS 131 information is more useful on average even accounting for companies that aggregate broadly. Net empirical picture: SFAS 131 jelas merupakan peningkatan dibandingkan SFAS 14.

**Citations**: Street, Nichols & Gray (2000); Herrmann & Thomas (2000); Wolk et al. (2017)

---

### Section V — Aplikasi: ICBP dan PSAK 5

**Purpose**: Ground the theoretical argument in Indonesian practice. Validate improvement; illustrate remaining limitation concretely.

**Konteks regulasi**
PSAK 5 (Segmen Operasi) = Indonesia's adoption of IFRS 8 = international equivalent of SFAS 131. Lineage: SFAS 131 (1997) → IFRS 8 (2006) → PSAK 5 (DSAK IAI). Management approach principle preserved in full.

**Ilustrasi keunggulan**
ICBP reports 6 segments: Mie Instan, Produk Susu, Penyedap Makanan, Makanan Ringan, Minuman, Pinehill International — corresponding to CODM's internal monitoring structure. Under SFAS 14-type approach, ICBP could plausibly have reported as single "food and beverage" entity. Management approach forces disclosure of economically distinct operations: Mie Instan (dominant domestic, high-margin) vs. Pinehill (international, includes Nigeria currency risk) have fundamentally different risk-return profiles. This is the informative disclosure value: analysts can assess performance and risk by meaningful economic unit.

**Ilustrasi keterbatasan (consistent with Section IV)**
Pinehill segment aggregates 12+ countries with dramatically different macroeconomic profiles (Nigeria devaluation, Saudi regulatory environment, Turkish inflation). Under PSAK 5, this aggregation is permitted because ICBP's CODM internally monitors Pinehill as a single unit. Country-level profitability is not disclosed. This concretely illustrates the Hermann & Thomas aggregation loophole: the management approach's strength (alignment with internal reporting) is also its limitation (disclosure quality bounded by organizational structure choices, not accounting choices). Improvement is real; it is not unlimited.

**Citations**: IAI PSAK 5 (2020); ICBP Annual Report 2024; Wolk et al. (2017)

---

### Section VI — Kesimpulan

**Purpose**: Converge the three argument threads. No new arguments.

**Sintesis 1 — Diagnosis dan koreksi struktural**
SFAS 14's core failure was definitional flexibility — compliance-driven, not communication-driven. SFAS 131 corrected this at the structural level by substituting the CODM's observable internal organization for unconstrained management choice. This is a reconception of what segment disclosure is *for*, not a technical refinement.

**Sintesis 2 — Validasi empiris**
The improvement is empirically confirmed: Street/Nichols/Gray show more granular reporting; predictive validity research shows more useful information. Hermann/Thomas aggregation caveat refines the verdict rather than reversing it: SFAS 131 is a genuine improvement whose quality is bounded by organizational structure choices, not accounting choices.

**Sintesis 3 — Implikasi kebijakan dan relevansi Indonesia**
SFAS 131's management approach — adopted globally as IFRS 8, in Indonesia as PSAK 5 — represents the culmination of the shift from protective to informative disclosure that Wolk identifies as the defining direction of modern disclosure policy. ICBP's six-segment structure under PSAK 5 demonstrates this in practice. The question answers itself through the standard's design logic: segment disclosure serves users' actual decision needs rather than management's definitional convenience.
